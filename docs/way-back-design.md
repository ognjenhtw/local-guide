# The way back: "your turn to be the local"

On the way there the guide leads. On the way back the roles switch: the walker leads and the
guide follows, like a friend who's along for the walk. The guide knows the way and never lets
the walker get lost, but it doesn't give the way away.

## Why

- **Recall, not following.** Saying the way out loud ("left at the rotary") is retrieval
  practice, and explaining it to someone else is learning by teaching. Both strengthen memory
  more than quietly following directions.
- **Trust, not silence.** The guide stays present and talking. It trusts the walker's lead and
  shows that by asking less once they've proved they know the way. The walker trusts the guide
  as a safety net that is always there.
- **Help without a test.** Help comes as the guide's own memory ("wait, didn't we pass a
  pharmacy?"), never as a teacher correcting someone.

## Rules (decided by the app, the same for every participant)

| Moment | What the guide does |
|---|---|
| Handover (Head back, or "let's head back") | One light line of its own that hands over the lead. No directions. |
| Coming up to a turn | Asks like a follower: "Which way here, boss?" Never hints. |
| They say a direction | Right: goes with it, or shares a memory of the spot. Wrong: only voices doubt ("you sure?"). It never says the right way here. |
| They're unsure / ask for help / stand still 12 s near a turn | Help step 1: asks what they remember. Step 2: a landmark hint, as its own memory. Step 3: the way at that turn. Counted per turn. |
| Two turns in a row right without help | Stops asking at turns and just follows and chats (fading). Any help turns asking back on. |
| Wrong way (off the route and getting farther from home) | 1: doubt ("hm, this doesn't look familiar"). After 40 s: 2, a hint. After 40 s more: 3, plainly how to get back on track. It never offers to lead. |
| "You lead" / "just tell me the way" | Only then does it lead the rest of the way (logged as a rescue). |
| "Let's go right instead" | At most 2 detours, each re-planned; after that it lightly sticks with the current way. |
| Back at the start | One natural line about a real moment (no corny praise), then: "What helped you most finding the way?" |

## The screen on the way back

- No route line and no checkpoint pictures.
- The map is folded away. Opened, it shows only the walker's own landmarks: no route, no
  position. A "Where am I?" button shows their position (the just-in-case).
- Every map open and every position shown is counted.

## What is recorded

Per turn: whether it was asked, help steps used (0 to 3), wrong-way moments, and the outcome
(on your own / with help, step N / after a wrong turn / guided). For the whole walk back: time,
detours, rescue, map opens, positions shown, the full GPS path and the transcript. The summary
is shown on screen at the end and saved in the walk log.
